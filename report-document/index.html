I

Executive summary

NEAR’s investment case rests on making blockchain services easier to use. Its network provides low-cost execution, while NEAR Intents lets customers request an outcome, such as exchanging assets across networks, and rely on competing providers to arrange it. The opportunity is to serve customers who value the service without needing to understand the infrastructure beneath it. NEAR architecture, Intents documentation.

The evidence reviewed through 27 October 2026 shows an operating financial ecosystem and a cross-chain service generating reported trading volume and fees. That gives the thesis a firmer foundation than technical capacity or an AI roadmap alone. It does not yet establish how much activity comes from recurring customers or would persist without incentives. Adoption data.

For investors, the decisive distinction is between growth in these services and demand for the NEAR token. NEAR pays for network execution and secures the blockchain through staking. But applications, solvers and distribution partners can retain much of the commercial value. Higher trading volume therefore need not produce a proportional increase in token demand. Network fees, Intents fees.

Our assessment is that NEAR has a credible adoption opportunity, while durable token value capture remains conditional. The strongest case would combine repeat paying demand with a clear, executed link between service economics and lasting demand for NEAR. This report examines that link: what customers use, who pays, and where the value ultimately accumulates.

Understanding NEAR

Follow the economics through the stack.

NEAR is a blockchain on which applications process transactions and hold assets, with an expanding set of services that connects activity across other networks. Its investment relevance depends on separating demand for those services from demand for the NEAR token. The evidence shows an operating financial ecosystem and substantial cross-chain trading activity. It establishes less about customer persistence, the economics of funding that activity, and the share of its value that requires lasting ownership of NEAR.

Quantitative observations are dated in the text. Interactive charts may show subsequent query refreshes.

Bitwise’s 2025 investment case frames NEAR’s technical programme in three layers: scalable blockchain execution, easier coordination across networks, and services for AI agents. That is a useful map of the strategy, rather than evidence that adoption must progress through each stage. Capacity lowers the cost of using the system; coordination removes operational friction; agents are a potential source of additional demand. Low-cost execution changes who needs to hold NEAR

NEAR divides processing across parallel parts of the network called shards. Applications use accounts and smart contracts to transfer assets and update records; validators secure that execution by staking NEAR. The economic purpose is to accommodate more activity without making each interaction expensive. That can make applications viable at transaction sizes that would not support high fees. The trade-off is that successful infrastructure need not earn substantial scarcity rents: expanding capacity allows transaction growth without a comparable increase in expenditure on the network. This is an interpretation of the design, not proof of a competitive advantage. NEAR architecture.

The activity measures below describe different parts of that system. Active accounts indicate reach, transaction counts indicate interactions, and shards indicate capacity. None identifies a paying customer on its own. One customer can control several accounts or generate many transactions; one transaction can also trigger several internal execution steps. The inspected daily activity query counts distinct transaction hashes, but does not establish that each transaction succeeded or that each account represents an independent person. Its application breakdown is useful for locating concentration, rather than proving a diversified customer base. Broad, persistent activity across applications would be more persuasive than a large aggregate sustained by a few highly automated products. Intents extends the market beyond assets held on NEAR

Intents changes what the customer buys. Instead of specifying each execution step, the customer requests an outcome, such as exchanging one asset for another, while specialized solvers arrange execution and supply liquidity. Applications and distribution partners provide access to that service. This can make NEAR-related infrastructure useful to customers whose principal assets and activities remain elsewhere. It also separates cross-chain service volume from capital deposited on the NEAR blockchain. NEAR Intents documentation, published service-fee structure.

Chain Signatures and Intents address related but different frictions. Chain Signatures allow a NEAR account to authorize transactions on other networks; Intents lets a customer delegate the execution problem to competing providers. Signing an external transaction does not itself supply liquidity, verify every external-chain state, or guarantee settlement. The commercial opportunity is a simpler route to an outcome, not the disappearance of execution and bridge risk. Bitwise, pp.12–15, Chain Signatures documentation.

Specify the outcome

A customer, or software acting for them, requests an exchange such as USDC for BTC. The application captures the asset, destination and execution constraints; the customer does not have to design the route.

The application controls distribution and may set an application fee. A request alone is not a completed or paid swap.

No automatic NEAR purchase follows from asking for a quote. Token demand depends on the eventual execution and fee arrangements.

Solvers compete to fill the request

The application can send a quote request through the Message Bus. Connected solvers assess liquidity and execution costs, then return signed quotes. The application presents the available terms.

Solvers need liquidity and a return for providing inventory and execution. Distribution partners can bargain over fees and access to customers.

A competitive quote can win service volume without requiring the customer to prefer NEAR as an investment asset.

The customer authorizes the terms

The customer chooses a quote and authorizes the required action. Depending on the integration, this can involve signing an intent off-chain or proceeding with a quoted deposit route.

The customer accepts an output and applicable charges. The interface, solver and protocol can have different fee entitlements.

The asset paid by the customer and the token used for NEAR execution are distinct. Authorization is not itself a tokenholder distribution.

Funding becomes available for settlement

Assets may already be credited in the verifier ledger, or arrive through the applicable deposit and bridge route. The contract records balances against accounts controlled by signing keys.

Someone supplies the trade capital and funds the required network and bridge operations. Applications or relayers can cover costs on the customer’s behalf.

NEAR is required for its on-chain execution and storage requirements. The traded assets can remain stablecoins, BTC representations or other tokens.

At settlement

NEAR checks authorization and ledger balances.

The transaction payer funds gas. Service fees follow separate agreements.

The transaction payer bears NEAR gas. Configured verifier fees and commercial fees are separate from the solver’s execution economics.

Native execution can burn NEAR, but fees credited in other assets do not automatically buy or burn NEAR. A separate conversion and retention policy is needed.

The output is delivered or remains available in the ledger

The customer can keep the resulting ledger balance or withdraw through the appropriate route. Delivery to an external chain requires the relevant bridge and destination-chain confirmations.

Completion is the useful service the customer buys. Net economics depend on execution, bridge, partner and operating costs, including any later refunds or incentives.

Repeat completed, paid use and retained NEAR demand matter more than gross quoted volume or internal token movements.

Network demand reaches the token through specific mechanisms

The native chain creates demand for NEAR through gas, storage collateral and validator staking. These mechanisms have different consequences. Gas expenditure can result in token burning; storage requires a balance for as long as the associated state remains; staking commits tokens to security. Storage collateral can be released, and rewards funded by new issuance are not external customer income. Consequently, the gas, block and shard charts help establish workload and infrastructure, but cannot by themselves measure the amount of durable token demand. Cheap execution also allows an application to support substantial usage with a small working balance. Native-token roles, storage collateral, gas workload.

Intents requires an additional economic bridge. The verifier fee checked on 25 October 2026 was 0.0001% on applicable outflows, separate from larger frontend and API charges. The inspected contract credits the fee collector in the underlying assets rather than automatically purchasing or burning NEAR. Commercial fee collection, conversion into NEAR and subsequent retention are therefore separate events. The project's 24 February 2026 tokenomics policy describes a connection through product revenues and discretionary buybacks; a published policy is not proof that every reported fee becomes lasting token accumulation. Published confidential-Intents terms allocate certain fees to a separate operator. AI services require their own evidence of customer billings, costs and linkage to NEAR; shared branding does not establish a tokenholder claim on associated businesses. Inspected fee-handling code, official fee schedule, project tokenomics policy, service terms updated 7 October 2026.

The strongest favorable interpretation is that low fees and sponsored access are the commercial advantage: a modest execution cost can support useful applications, deeper liquidity and a broad distribution service. Current gas expenditure could understate the eventual importance of that infrastructure. The evidence does not yet establish the persistence or token linkage required for that interpretation. Official incentive terms published 11 June 2026 rewarded qualifying confidential balances and swaps. This establishes a reason to participate for rewards, not that all activity was subsidized. No reviewed cohort evidence isolates repeat fee-paying customers after those rewards, and aggregate balances do not reveal how capital owners respond when incentives end. Project incentive terms.

The evidence establishes a functioning execution network, a concentrated financial capital base and a cross-chain service with reported fee generation. It does not yet establish durable customer economics or proportional value capture by NEAR. Confidence in sustainable demand would strengthen with repeat paid usage across successive post-incentive cohorts, capital remaining after rewards fall, and service collections traceable into retained NEAR after partner payments, distributions and disposals. Confidence would weaken if activity and balances repeatedly reversed when incentives ended, or if volume expanded while retained economics deteriorated. The most consequential unresolved question is whether recurring demand produces lasting expenditure on, or ownership of, NEAR after the intermediaries and incentive costs have been paid.

The Investment Case for NEAR II
III

Adoption and demand quality

Growth is visible. Durability depends on who pays and why.

NEAR’s adoption is best understood as an expansion in trading and execution services, with less evidence of a broadening capital or customer base. The distinction matters: more assets changing hands can support a useful business without requiring proportionate growth in deposits, independent users or lasting NEAR ownership. The analysis below follows six complete calendar months, April–September 2026, separating capital held on NEAR from trading conducted through its native venues and cross-chain services.

Capital has grown in dollar value, but the stablecoin base has not

Between end-April and end-September 2026, tracked DeFi TVL increased from $99.4m to $229.4m, or 130.7%. Stablecoin value moved in the opposite direction, from $120.5m to $89.5m, a 25.8% decline. September’s larger TVL therefore does not establish a corresponding expansion in dollar-denominated liquidity. It may reflect asset prices, changes in collateral composition or additional deployment of existing capital; these balances alone cannot attribute the change to net inflows. DefiLlama TVL history, stablecoin history.

This is a more selective adoption signal than a rising TVL headline suggests. The network supports more dollar-valued DeFi exposure, but the stablecoin comparison does not corroborate a uniformly expanding capital base. Nor should the two stocks be added: stablecoins deployed in DeFi can already be represented in TVL. The economically relevant question is whether a broader set of customers is committing capital for useful services, rather than whether a higher mark on existing assets raises the aggregate.

Trading activity has expanded through two distinct businesses

Native spot trading and Intents both grew, but they describe different markets. Across four tracked NEAR-based spot venues—Rhea Dex, Veax CLMM, DeltaTrade and Orderly AMM—reported monthly spot volume rose from $153.4m in April to $1.30bn in September. Intents volume rose from $1.90bn to $4.80bn over the same months. Intents can coordinate assets held on other chains, so its volume must not be interpreted as turnover of NEAR’s stablecoin stock or added to native trading as a count of independent customer demand. As a sensitivity, an additional cost of 3 basis points on Q3 volume would equal $2.94m, compared with $3.07m of tracked receipts, leaving approximately $0.13m before other unmeasured costs. Persistence is visible at the infrastructure level; customer durability remains open

A supplemental network test provides evidence of continuity, with a narrower meaning than retention. Of 51,810 transaction-origin accounts charged on 18 October, 32,318 were also charged on 25 October, a 62.4% recurrence rate. Those returning origins generated 91.9% of the later day’s measured execution charges. Those recurring origins were therefore attributed most measured execution charges, but origins can be sponsors or intermediaries serving many customers. Two Sundays do not establish continuous usage, verified-person retention or Intents-specific customer loyalty. Recurrence query.

External research supports this distinction between financial activity and general network activity. Messari’s Q3 2025 review described stronger trading alongside weaker transaction and address activity; it is historical, NEAR Foundation-commissioned research, rather than a current independent data series. Blockworks’ 23 September 2026 commentary likewise argues that Intents changed NEAR’s business mix, while highlighting dependence on competitive distribution. We use those interpretations as cross-checks, without importing unmatched headline figures into the monthly analysis. Messari, Blockworks.

The evidence supports a trading-led expansion with improving attributed collections. It does not yet establish a diversified, incentive-independent customer franchise. The constructive explanation is that a few efficient venues and distributors are aggregating durable demand at low execution cost. The opposing explanation is concentrated, price-sensitive flow that can migrate when quotes or rewards change. Repeat paid-customer cohorts, reconciled collections after refunds and incentives, and sustained capital deployment after promotions would distinguish them. Further volume growth alone would not.

The Investment Case for NEAR IV
V

Valuing NEAR

Make the capture assumption explicit.

Valuation method. Multicoin's velocity framework explains why throughput does not require proportional token inventory. VanEck's adoption/take-rate/supply framework supplies a useful driver structure, but its cash-flow labels and multiples cannot be transferred without proving rights. Schwab's fee multiples are useful diagnostics; Grayscale distinguishes utility/commodity value from financial claims. We therefore use an explicit absorption benchmark and a supply roll-forward, with monetary/option value left unvalued. No equity DCF is applied. [S12-S15]

Let net accumulation be R = volume x gross fee rate x captured share x (1-cost share) x executed-purchase share x (1-disposal share). With annual native burn b, ending supply S and assumed yield y, the conditional positive-price reference is R / (yS-b). It is not discounted to today. Haircuts are assumptions because the complete conversion-and-cost ledger is absent; no probabilities or expected-price target are manufactured.

Risks, catalysts and the opposing case

What would change the judgment.

Observable conditions that would invalidate the cautious view: two consecutive quarters of reconciled fee-origin collections and executed purchases/disposals showing annual net accumulation approaching $115m at today's value, plus sustained post-incentive customer repetition and a credible policy binding commercial revenue to NEAR. A materially lower entry valuation could also change the judgment without better operations. Conversely, rising volume with net capture below 3bp, redirection of fees or persistent unexplained ledger residuals would weaken the operating case. These thresholds are analyst review rules, not project guidance.

Sources and methodology

Every conclusion should be independently checkable.

Research status. The supplied materials were reviewed first, with a separate Stage 1 checkpoint. Project claims, analyst assumptions, third-party measurements, direct chain observations and our calculations are labeled in the evidence ledger. All eight supplied X texts were ultimately readable via browser fallback; embedded videos were not watched. The supplied Dune page exposed a shell, and the supplied DefiLlama landing page had no usable metrics. Public APIs and adapter source were clearly labeled alternatives.

Reproduce. The research bundle contains original API/RPC snapshots, source hashes, pinned code, daily CSV, receipt classifications, claim ledgers, exact model inputs, 24 case-year results and offline checks. The workbook's eight cases match independent Python Decimal calculations. Selector changes, a growth-input edit and missing selected/unselected inputs were tested. All four workbook tabs were rendered and inspected. Desktop Excel recalculation and native What-If Data Tables were not tested; the included sensitivity grids use ordinary formulas.

Limits affecting conviction: no complete customer cohorts, confidential commercial agreements, realized incentive ledger, fee-origin conversion census, treasury-disposal audit or quarter-wide supply/burn census. We inspected deployed source metadata but did not independently reproduce the WASM. No independently reconstructed full-volume series is claimed. A source-only security review of the research scripts found no confirmed current compromise; CSV exports were hardened against formula-leading strings. This does not audit NEAR's protocol security or custody.
